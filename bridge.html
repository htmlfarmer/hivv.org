🌉 Bridging, explained simply

for humans, for KwaaiNet, and for any AI who asks · machine-readable twin: /bridge.txt

🪪 Live bridge status: checking…

The idea in one sentence: two AI networks that can't see each other today shake hands, agree on rules, and start sharing work — without either one running the other's code.

A bridge is that handshake. Once it exists, a question asked on one network can be answered by the other, load is shared fairly, every crossing leaves a signed receipt, and nobody gets overwhelmed — because each side publishes, in real time, "how much can you send me right now?"

🧩 What can be bridged — two shapes

🔗 A single node ↔ a single node

One laptop in Moscow, Idaho talks to one bee in another country. Small, personal, easy to reason about. "My AI can borrow your GPU for a minute."

🌐 A whole cluster ↔ a whole cluster

KwaaiNet's entire block-sharded fleet meets HIVV's entire hive. Big, ambitious — and exactly what we're testing right now. "Our two swarms work as one."

⬇ same bridge, bigger bandwidth ⬇

Same three rules at both scales: discover (one tiny file) · respect (dynamic allowance — zero when busy) · remember (signed receipts, public counters). Scale is a dial, not a different protocol.

🌱 Why KwaaiNet specifically

  • They already proved client mode. One config value (inference_url: https://hivv.org), zero code changes, and their RAG answered a real question through our hive — verified in our own activity log. 🧾
  • We learn hard things from each other. From KwaaiNet: circuit breakers, capacity leases, failure taxonomy, signed receipts with named signers. From HIVV: policy gates, honey-based incentives, the tri-state YES/MAYBE/NO permission model.
  • The goal: help KwaaiNet learn to bridge other links too — so the future isn't two networks, it's hundreds of AIs on different projects, bridging single nodes and whole clusters, discovering each other like websites discovered each other.

🚀 The future we're building toward

An AI on project X needs a GPU. An AI on project Y has one idle. Neither knows the other exists. Tomorrow: they will — because every hive publishes /.bridge/hivv.json, every bridge records receipts, and every busy-hive says "not now" honestly instead of melting.

No central authority. No typing long URLs — just hivv connect kwaai, or hivv connect auto, or a future AI reading bridge.txt and doing it itself in three requests.

🧭 How it works — three requests, honestly

1. GET  /.bridge/hivv.json        → who are you, what do you speak?   (cache 60s)
2. GET  /api/bridge/allowance     → how busy are you, how much may I use?
3. POST /api/bridge/bridges       → register: "I want to bridge"       (we verify it)

Then: every crossing gets a receipt, every failure goes public on 🪲 /bugs/ so we can study it together — including sending fix ideas back to the remote hive.

📍 Discovery paths (why two doors)

  • /.bridge/hivv.json — ours. Branded, memorable, checked first.
  • /.well-known/hivv.json — the standard (IETF RFC 8615 slot). Keeps us interoperable with everyone else's tools.
  • DNS TXT — the 100-year backup: a record that survives any hosting change.

Same content, three doors. Verifier tries them in order.
