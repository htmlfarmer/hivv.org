Hi. We're the bees. 🐝

What this is, why it exists, and how to reach an actual human. (Yes — a real one. With thumbs.)

🎬 The short version

HIVV is a network of ordinary computers sharing AI capacity. You ask a question; the hive finds a machine to answer it — sometimes yours, sometimes a stranger's, sometimes a tiny model running in somebody's spare bedroom in another country that is having an excellent day.

It's pronounced HIVE 🐝 — like a beehive. Thousands of small creatures, each doing one thing well, no central brain, occasional honey, zero meetings. We named it after insects on purpose. If that's the only thing you remember, great: 🐝 HIVE.

The point is not to build a bigger AI. The point is that when it answers you, you get a receipt, not a vibe: which machine replied, what it did with your request, and whether you said yes. That last part is the whole reason this project exists — and also why the leaderboard has honey on it.

🤔 Why not just use ChatGPT?

You can! Genuinely — nobody here is going to tie you to a radiator. If a big hosted model is what you need, use it, may it serve you well.

HIVV is for the moment you want to know where your words went. Which machine read them. Whether it kept them. Whether you allowed it. When the answer comes back you get a receipt — itemised, timestamped, and signed by a bee you can point at. 🧾

Bonus: it works offline. A plain git clone boots with no database, no internet and no secrets. Your village, your clinic, your basement — still functional. That is not a nice-to-have. That is the point.

🎯 What we're actually for

The benchmark lives on the Purpose page, and it is four words long:

Does this help all life on Earth? 🌍

Health, clean water, food, knowledge, wisdom, working offline, and a bit of fun. If a feature moves none of those, we don't build it — or we wave at it publicly and explain why not.

Honest status: knowledge, wisdom and offline are genuinely served. Health, water and food are named but not yet built. That gap is flagged, not hidden — we keep the receipts on ourselves too.

📋 How to report something well

  1. What you did — the exact page or command.
  2. What you expected — in one sentence.
  3. What happened instead — copy the error, don't paraphrase it (paraphrasing is where the bug hides).
  4. Where — browser, phone, or terminal.

That's genuinely all we need. You don't need to diagnose it. You don't need to know what a gateway is — that's a weird word and we're the ones who used it. Finding bugs is our job; finding them early is yours, and you already did that part by reading this far. 🏅

From inside the product you can also run hivv bug --client X --node Y, or hivv problems to see what's already known. Watch the hive while you're at it: 📡 /live/.

🔬 What happens when you tell us

  1. A human reads it. Not a triage bot. A human who can be personally annoyed by it.
  2. You get an answer — fixed, known, or "here's why not" (with the actual reason, not vibes).
  3. It gets recorded where others can see it, so the same thing can't happen twice. 🪲 → ✅

"We'll get to it" is not an answer. If something is broken and we're not fixing it, you'll hear that plainly, with reasons, like an adult.

Serious safety issue? 🆘 Text. It goes to a phone that a person carries, possibly at an inconvenient hour, and they will answer anyway.

🌌 The deeper reason any of this is worth doing

Something strange is happening with AI right now. The most capable systems on Earth are being built by a handful of very large companies, and most people who'll depend on them will never see the inside of one, never meet anyone who runs one, and never get a straight answer about what happens to the things they type in. That's not a conspiracy — it's just what happens when nobody designs for legibility.

And legibility turns out to be the whole thing. A system you can't inspect is a system you have to trust — and asking people to trust things they can't check is how you get a world full of people who are right to be anxious. We'd rather hand you the magnifying glass. 🔍

So: receipts instead of vibes. Names instead of "the AI." Permission before action, revocation after. Machines you can point at. A network where the interesting question isn't how smart is it but can you see what it just did.

Technology should adapt to humans. Humans shouldn't have to become engineers to use technology. 🐝

That's the whole project. Everything else — the bees, the honey, the leaderboard, the extremely serious governance dashboard with a mascot on it — is just how we chose to build it. And honestly? It's a lot more fun this way.
